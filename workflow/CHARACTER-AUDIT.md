# Unslop review — beyond obvious effects

Use the installed Unslop skill, including its signs and relevant reference material, before checkpoint 2 and final delivery. This project adaptation supplements its review rather than modifying upstream files. The brief, explicit character preference and selected direction take precedence. An expressive design may legitimately use gradients, layering or bold colors. Their presence alone is not a defect. Removing all effects does not establish quality or identity.

## Examine the product, not a checklist of banned decoration

- **Interchangeability:** if the name/logo were replaced, what visible decisions would still belong to this product's task and content? Identify actual evidence rather than claiming uniqueness.
- **Repeated recipe:** are identical pills, radii, shadows, panels or spacing patterns used regardless of role? Reuse is useful; hierarchy should still distinguish navigation, data, action and detail.
- **Authored hierarchy:** is there a purposeful focal area and a clear scan path, or does every section have equal visual weight? Richness should support selected content rather than cover the entire shell automatically.
- **Spatial logic:** do anchors, inset relationships and alignment axes explain where elements sit? Inspect sidebar footer/identity, action groups, chart edges and panel content; absence of overflow is insufficient.
- **Subject-specific graphics:** do charts, illustrations and icons clarify this product? Spot meaningless visual filler, unsupported smoothing, generic waves and fake data, not just glow/emoji.
- **Copy:** remove repeated explanatory filler, generic headings and boilerplate when a concise contextual definition or tooltip would serve better. Preserve essential units, caveats and denominators.

## Check specific layers in FigCraft

Use the signals below to locate concrete fixes in the existing Unslop pass. Apply each check where it fits the task and selected character.

- **Competing emphasis:** trace the strongest fills, gradients, shadows and actions across the first viewport. If the navigation, repeated buttons or decorative surfaces compete with the main content, reduce their emphasis and recheck where the eye lands. Where two controls lead to the same action, make the contextual entry point primary and review whether the other entry point still helps.
- **Repetition and optical finish:** compare recurring cards and controls at 100%. Trace gaps and radii back to their roles or shared tokens. Recheck nested corners, repeated icon containers, and small tracked uppercase labels where they weaken hierarchy or legibility. Use values that fit the chosen system and content density.
- **Content that earns the space:** read headlines, card labels, badges and metrics in context. Replace generic claims with specific product language, remove unsupported proof, and keep real metrics near what they describe. Inspect prominent cards and overlays for the information or action they add. Rework oversized containers around the content they need to hold.

Use the existing audit record for the affected frame/layer, observed problem, fix and repeated screenshot. ## Turn the audit into design decisions

- **Recognizable language:** name the product/task relationship behind the visual device, then point to its expression on both sample screens. Swapping the name is a useful diagnostic, not proof that every shared convention is wrong. Familiar controls and library icons are desirable; the authored language should come from meaningful composition, typography, content graphics or states, not redrawing standard icons.
- **Earned space and interaction:** inspect sparse large cards and overlays. Ask what hierarchy the space creates and what opening the detail actually adds: useful information, closer inspection, comparison or an action. A large dialog that merely repeats its trigger card and offers Close is a finding when it interrupts without adding value. Improve the use of existing content or choose inline disclosure/appropriate scale; do not invent features or data to fill space. Deliberate breathing room remains valid.
- **Reader-facing copy:** distinguish essential interpretation (units, denominator, scope, estimates or missing data) from audit provenance, repeated formulas and implementation notes. Keep the former visible where needed. Move secondary definitions to a designed accessible help/disclosure state, and keep verification details in project records. Do not remove safety-relevant ambiguity by hiding all caveats.
- **Graphic craft:** correct arithmetic and axes establish analytical validity, not finish. Inspect mark weight, reference-line contrast, numeric typography, label spacing, legend relationships and coordinated selection/detail treatment. Refine appropriate properties within the chosen language. Preserve raw values, meaningful scales and missing states; never fabricate peaks, apply misleading smoothing or add decorative series for excitement.

These checks apply to every product category. Chart-specific criteria apply only where there are charts; other products may express identity through imagery, editorial composition or meaningful product states. Existing content is the material, not a requirement to create extra screens or novelty.

## Act proportionally and preserve the direction

For each finding record the layer/screen, observable problem, effect on hierarchy or understanding, proposed change and a repeated screenshot check. Do not report only “no gradients/emoji found”. If no actual issue exists, state what was examined without inventing a redesign. Routine polish within the approved direction is authorized by the design task; radical changes still require discussion.

Treat “AI-looking” as subjective feedback to translate into concrete decisions, not as a measurable detection claim. Audit axes are fit, craft, hierarchy and recognizable visual authorship. Compare against examined references and the approved samples. Do not add arbitrary eccentricities solely to appear less generic.

Report unresolved issues honestly. Reading Unslop, counting its use or passing structural checks does not prove this review was effective. Do not claim a percentage of “AI removed”. In this project, review/edit FigCraft layers and screenshots; do not reconstruct HTML or run its browser/video workflow.
