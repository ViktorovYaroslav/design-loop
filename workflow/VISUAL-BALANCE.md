# Visual balance — choose proportions for the task

Choose a working color-area budget in DIRECTION when developing the palette, then inspect it on the actual screen. Distinguish dominant/base surfaces, secondary surfaces and emphasis. This is a composition heuristic, not a universal three-color limit or a proof of quality.

| Starting hypothesis | Possible use |
|---|---|
| 60 / 30 / 10 dominant, secondary, accent | A visibly differentiated composition with substantial secondary surfaces. |
| 80 / 15 / 5 dominant, secondary, accent | A quiet workspace with modest surface variation and selective emphasis. |
| 90–95% neutral field / 5–10% emphasis | A largely neutral product where content and a few actions/signals carry attention. |

Only 60/30/10 here is the conventional guideline discussed by the linked source. The other rows are project working hypotheses, not ranked scientific laws or a verified Vercel specification. The two-role model is not directly equivalent to the three-role one. Choose based on content, desired attention and character, not by product category alone. All three can support expressive or restrained work through type, shape, imagery and finish.

Percentages describe approximate visible area, not token counts, saturation or perceived visual weight. A tiny saturated warning can dominate a large neutral surface. Count gradients and imagery by their practical visual role; do not pretend a casual screenshot review is an exact pixel measurement. If a percentage is reported as measured, document viewport, region/masks, treatment of text/images and method. Otherwise label it an estimate or omit numbers.

Check first focus, contrast, competing accents, neutral resting areas and consistency across screens. Give chart series, selected controls, brand accents and semantic warnings distinct rules; a color budget must not erase meaningful series or force warnings into the brand color. Inspect difficult states where many alerts/data series appear. Color alone must not carry an essential distinction.

Use spacing tiers, type scale and content-driven column widths as separate systems. Do not apply a palette-area ratio to sidebar width, card counts, typography or whitespace. Golden-ratio layouts and modular scales are optional tools, not required formulas. An asymmetric composition needs a visible hierarchy and relational alignment, not a numeric justification alone.

Source: [NN/g — Using Color to Enhance Your Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/color-enhance-design/), checked 2026-11-15. It recommends applying 60/30/10, then iterating for hierarchy, consistency and legibility. Compliance alone does not guarantee a good interface. No prior experiment's proportions were measured or certified by this update.
